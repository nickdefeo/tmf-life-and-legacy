Two audiences, equally weighted

  • Prospective agents — the agency actively recruits, so the identity must also read as a credible employer.
Sober. Trustworthy. Permanent. Editorial.

People engage this brand while thinking about their own death and their children's future. It has to feel like an institution that will still be here in thirty years.

Not: playful, bouncy, startup-y, illustrative, clip-art, cartoon-rounded, or gradient-heavy. Restraint reads as trustworthy; decoration reads as inexperience.

Two reasons not to build the mark from lime

One: the brand direction — the scheme leads with blue and green, and yellow is held back. Two, the hard one: lime #DBE64C is invisible on white at 1.3:1, as the specimen above shows, so a lime-led mark disappears on letterhead, an invoice, a white slide, or any light section. Build the mark from blue and green, and supply two colourways.

Gradients

None in type, and none in the mark. An earlier revision put a lime-to-Mantis gradient on headlines and stat numerals; it was rejected for reading playful. Type is solid colour only, and the logo likewise should be flat solid fills — no gradients, bevels, drop shadows, or metallic effects.

Body, UI, buttons, nav, eyebrow labels Inter
400 body / 500–600 UI		 Eyebrows are uppercase, tracked 0.22em.

The wordmark should stay Cormorant Garamond so it matches the headlines. If you letter it custom, it has to sit comfortably beside Cormorant.

The logo

Delivered — in use on the site

A ligature lockup, 668×428 RGBA PNG, transparent, drawn in Praxeti White #F6F7ED with a Mantis #74C365 accent. It is live in the site header, footer and About panel.

On Midnight Mirage — as designed
Works. 15.3:1 for the white, 7.7:1 for the green.
On Praxeti White — the gap
Invisible. The lockup is drawn in the same white as this ground — 1.00:1. Only the small green accent survives, at 2.0:1.
Still outstanding

The navy colourway is done. What the brand still needs, in priority order:

  • A dark colourway for light grounds. The delivered file is white, so it disappears on letterhead, an invoice, a white slide, or any light section — as the panel above shows. Same lockup drawn in Midnight Mirage #001F3F with the Picture Book Green #00804C accent, which reads at 15.7:1 and 4.7:1.
  • An SVG of the lockup. The PNG is 668px wide, so it stays crisp to about 334 CSS px on a 2× display and 222px at 3×. That covers the current header and About uses, but it will soften on any large or print application.
  • A square mark for icon sizes. A wide ligature cannot read at 16×16 px. The favicon, the Apple touch icon and the social share card still carry a generated placeholder.
  • One-colour versions — solid navy and knockout white, for single-colour print, embroidery and engraving.
  • The mark on its own, without the wordmark, if the lockup has a separable element.

What the retired gold logo contributed

The original was gold: a serif TMF monogram framed by two laurel branches, an arch above, an eight-point star at the apex, and LIFE & LEGACY beneath a rule. Its equity was in the laurel, the star, the arch and the serif wordmark — useful context if any further variants are drawn.

Full deliverable set, for reference

  • Primary lockup — mark + TMF + rule + LIFE & LEGACY. Vertical preferred (it sits in the site header); a horizontal variant is welcome.
  • Mark alone — must be recognisable with no wordmark.
  • Favicon variant — a simplified separate drawing, not a scaled-down copy.
  • Two colourways — one for navy grounds (Mantis / Picture Book Green mark, Praxeti White wordmark), one for light grounds (Midnight Mirage navy and/or Picture Book Green).
  • One-colour versions — solid navy and knockout white, for single-colour print, embroidery and engraving.
  • SVG — flat fills, no embedded raster, no filters, modest path count. It gets inlined into a React component.

Hard requirements

  • Legible at 16×16 px. It ships as the browser favicon. This is the constraint that kills most detailed marks.
  • Works on navy and on Praxeti White, per the two colourways above.
  • Must not rely on a gradient, shadow or texture to read.
  • Wordmark legible at a header height of roughly 44px.

History — directions tried and rejected before the delivered mark

AttemptWhy it failed
Round tree — canopy of overlapping circles, white trunk Generic clip-art. A featureless mass with no negative space. Read as a landscaping company.
Radial leaf fan from a single point Read as a sunflower or daisy — mechanical radial petals around a hollow centre.
Tapered fan tree Read as a tulip. Flat-bottomed petal arrangement.
Laurel wreath with a vertical trunk through it The trunk — a straight shaft with a flared base — read as a sword or torch. Award and military iconography, wrong for life insurance. Three proportions tested; all had the problem.
Hollow laurel wreath with apex star Closest attempt and technically sound, but not good enough. Sits in the repo as a placeholder.
Two-tone leaves (lime outer, Mantis inner) The tonal difference is invisible below ~48px. Buys nothing, complicates the file.

The pattern in those failures: every literal-plant approach drifted toward a generic or unintentionally comic form. Kept here so that any further variants don't retrace the same ground.

Note that “Legacy” is doing real work in the name: generational continuity, inheritance and permanence are the concepts to mine, not “growth” in the startup sense.

The prompt

For the outstanding variants. Self-contained — hand it to a designer or paste it into another tool.

We have a delivered primary logo for TMF Life & Legacy, an independent life insurance agency (a d/b/a of National Life Advisors LLC) licensed in New York and Florida, based in Pompano Beach. Tagline: Protecting What Matters Most. The existing lockup is a ligature wordmark, 668×428 transparent PNG, drawn in Praxeti White #F6F7ED with a Mantis #74C365 accent, and it works on the brand's navy backgrounds. We need the missing variants of that same lockup — not a new logo.

Produce, in priority order: (1) a dark colourway of the identical lockup for light grounds, drawn in Midnight Mirage #001F3F with a Picture Book Green #00804C accent — the current file is white and is literally invisible on the brand's light background, at 1.00:1; (2) an SVG of the lockup in both colourways, since the PNG only stays crisp to about 334 CSS px on a 2× display; (3) a square mark that reads at 16×16 px for the favicon, app icon and social card — a wide ligature cannot survive that size, so this is a separate simplified drawing; (4) one-colour versions, solid navy and knockout white, for single-colour print, embroidery and engraving; and (5) the mark alone without the wordmark, if the lockup has a separable element.

Tone: sober, institutional, permanent, editorial. People engage this brand while thinking about their own death and their children's future. It must feel like an institution that will still exist in thirty years. Explicitly avoid playful, bouncy, startup-y, illustrative, clip-art, cartoon-rounded or gradient-heavy treatments. Restraint reads as trustworthy.

Use only this palette, and lead with blue and green: Midnight Mirage #001F3F (primary base), Nuit Blanche #1E488F, Praxeti White #F6F7ED, Mantis #74C365, Picture Book Green #00804C. First Colors of Spring #DBE64C is a brand colour but is held in reserve — it is used nowhere in the interface, and the mark should not lead with it. Flat solid fills only — no gradients, bevels, shadows or metallic effects.

Critical technical constraint: lime #DBE64C has only 1.3:1 contrast on Praxeti White and is invisible there; Mantis is only 2.0:1. Deliver two colourways — one for navy grounds (Mantis / Picture Book Green mark with a Praxeti White wordmark) and one for light grounds (Midnight Mirage navy / Picture Book Green). Also supply solid one-colour navy and knockout-white versions.

Match the delivered lockup exactly in letterforms, proportion and spacing — these are colourway and format variants of it, not reinterpretations. For the square icon, which does need a fresh drawing, the retired gold logo's vocabulary is the reference: a serif TMF monogram framed by laurel branches, an arch, and an eight-point star at the apex.

Wordmark: set TMF in Cormorant Garamond (500–600), the site's display face, with LIFE & LEGACY beneath a hairline rule in a tracked sans (Inter). Custom lettering is fine if it sits comfortably beside Cormorant.

Format: SVG, flat fills, no embedded raster, no filters, modest path count — the assets get inlined into a React component. Supply the square icon as SVG plus a 180×180 PNG.

For the square icon only, avoid these — they were tried during the original logo exploration and rejected: a round tree with a canopy of overlapping circles (generic clip-art); a radial leaf fan (read as a sunflower); a tapered fan (read as a tulip); and a laurel wreath with a vertical trunk through the middle (the trunk read as a sword or torch at every proportion tested). Every literal-plant approach drifted toward a generic or unintentionally comic form. A monogram-led icon built from the TMF letterforms, or a compact crest, suits the institutional tone better.

Mine the word “Legacy” — generational continuity, inheritance, permanence — rather than “growth” in the startup sense.
